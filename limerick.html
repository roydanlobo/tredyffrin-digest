August 4, 2026
Official minutes (PDF) Video archive
Public SafetyInfrastructureParks & RecreationFinance
  • Chief Matalavage retires. Chief of Police Robert J. Matalavage retired after almost 40 years of service and was approved for a Public Safety Specialist agreement.
  • Roche promoted to Chief. Lieutenant Patrick M. Roche was promoted to Chief of Police after 18+ years of service and sworn in by Judge Richard Welsh.
  • Bills totaling over $1 million approved. The Board unanimously approved Bills List #15 in the amount of $1,044,977.13.
  • Emerald Crossing projects advance. The Board authorized resolutions for two Emerald Crossing development projects at 142 W. Ridge Pike, with one passing 4-1 after discussion of sidewalks, stormwater, and traffic concerns; resident Martha Bell stated the intersection needs a traffic light.
  • Comprehensive Plan consultant selected. The Board approved Remington & Vernick Engineers, Inc. as consultant for the Comprehensive Plan update in the amount of $147,260; resident Martha Bell requested the Township confirm no conflicts of interest with current projects.
  • Township Line Road to close. PennDOT will close Township Line Road from August 17th through October 25th to replace the culvert over Lodal Creek.
  • Manderach Playground reopening. The Manderach Memorial Playground will have a Grand Re-opening on August 15th prior to Community Day.
July 28, 2026
Official minutes (PDF) Video archive
Zoning & DevelopmentFinanceParks & Recreation
  • Industrial development approved. The Board voted 4-1 to approve the Lot 7 Industrial Parkway project, with Supervisor Irwin opposed.
  • Indoor sports complex opposed. All supervisors expressed opposition to a proposed 85,500 square foot indoor sports complex at 18 Steinmetz Road, citing that the area is not zoned for this use and unauthorized tree clearing occurred on site.
  • Main Street District redesign discussed. Supervisors agreed to divide the existing Main Street District into three separate zoning areas, with support for mixed-use buildings (retail/restaurant on first floor, residential above) and opposition to more drug stores, drive-throughs, gas stations, and large apartment buildings on Ridge Pike.
  • Tax increase may be needed for 2027. During budget discussions, supervisors acknowledged that revenue is not keeping up with expenses due to inflation and need for additional services, with several board members saying they would support a tax increase for capital projects, fire and EMS, and ongoing inflationary costs.
  • Residents urged fiscal restraint. Residents Martha Bell and Rick Fidler asked the Board to keep taxes low and defer certain wants, while Tim Norwood urged the budget to prioritize Police and Fire.
  • Tricentennial under budget. The Township’s Tricentennial event will cost significantly less than the budgeted $75,000 due to successful fundraising efforts.
  • Community Park improvements underway. The rubberized playground surface is almost complete, a new fence will be installed, and a grand reopening is scheduled for Community Day on August 15th.

How this would work as a real product

Limerick runs on the same township website platform as Upper Merion (CivicPlus’s AgendaCenter), and its own quirk is worth knowing: rather than a single-meeting PDF per meeting, its newer postings bundle several meetings’ minutes, the current agenda, and unrelated exhibits into one large “Resident Packet” — the two entries above (and their official-minutes links) were both pulled from one such packet. Limerick has been in the local news recently over a proposed data center and its proximity to the Limerick Generating Station, which is exactly the kind of decision this project exists to make easier to follow — residents get the plain-language version of what the Board actually discussed and voted on, sourced straight from the official minutes.
