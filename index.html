It is meant as a fast starting point, not a replacement for the official record — always verify anything important against the source PDF or video before acting on it or repeating it as fact.

July 20, 2026
Official minutes (PDF) Video archive
Parks & Recreation Infrastructure Public Safety Finance
  • Property acquired. The board voted 6–0 to adopt Ordinance HR-497, approving the township’s acquisition of property at 599 Park Ridge Drive.
  • Split vote on a park event. A proposal for an “America 250 Reenactment Camp” in Teegarden Park (Sept 19–20, $3,000) passed 4–2. The two dissenting supervisors cited Park & Recreation Board concerns about overnight camping, safety, and lease complications.
  • Road & sewer work approved. $187,745 for a stormwater relining project on Upper Weadley Road, plus a $21,069.68 emergency sewer repair on Pugh Road.
  • A retirement, recognized. Officer Joseph Butler was honored for 24+ years of police service. Volunteer Stanford Nishikawa was also recognized.
  • Residents pushed back again. Concerns were raised about Wilson Farm Park renovation costs, and specifically about the Chase Road Park project’s accelerated timeline and rising expenses — see the storyline above.
June 15, 2026
Official minutes (PDF) Video archive
Parks & Recreation Infrastructure Governance
  • Chase Road Park hits a snag. Resident Carol Clarke asked the board to “pause the project and reconsider the costs and scope.” After discussion, the board voted 5–2 to postpone the Change Order #1 decision to the July 20 meeting.

  • Utility work. A pump station connection installation was approved for $29,800, and $64,565.80 in escrow was released for the Berwyn Village development.
  • Recordkeeping. An addition was made to the township’s Historic Resources Map.
  • Housekeeping. Township offices announced closures for Juneteenth (June 19) and Independence Day observances (July 2–3), and the Chester County Planning Commission presented the township with an official America250PA Chester County flag.
May 20, 2026
Official minutes (PDF) Video archive
Parks & Recreation Infrastructure Governance Public Safety
  • Chase Road Park kicks off. The board awarded a $1.77 million contract for Chase Road Park upgrades — the project that would draw resident pushback the following month.
  • Charter change adopted. Following a public hearing, the board voted 6–0 to adopt Ordinance HR-496, amending the Township’s Home Rule Charter.
  • Major projects greenlit. $1.17 million for 2026 road paving (Superpave), a $1.33 million TASA grant for Valley Forge Road pedestrian/sidewalk improvements, $24,370 for emergency culvert repairs, and a $147,200 design contract for sanitary sewer main upgrades.
  • Appointments. Michele Burger joined the Park & Recreation Board; Mark Dixon and Pearl Nudy were reappointed to the Historical Commission; Kim Tempas joined the Environmental Advisory Council.
  • Recognitions. Sergeant Kevin Moore was honored for 46 years of police service; Officer Brittany Rosenfeld received Officer of the Year.
  • Student housing concerns. Residents raised issues about student housing in the Mt. Pleasant neighborhood; supervisors committed to working with Villanova University and updating enforcement ordinances.

April 20, 2026
Official minutes (PDF) Video archive
Governance Public Safety Finance Infrastructure
  • Charter amendment adopted. Following a public hearing, the board voted 7–0 to adopt Ordinance HR-495 amending the Home Rule Charter’s Chapter 4 governance provisions, and scheduled a May 20 public hearing for a related amendment, Ordinance HR-496.
  • Mental health and policing. Resident Carol Matthews pushed for Pennsylvania “red flag” laws and raised concerns about police response to mental health crises; Jessica Tinneny asked the board to consider adding a mental health specialist to police staff.
  • Data centers on the table. The board authorized staff to begin drafting zoning amendments specifically for data centers, after resident Margaret van Naerssen urged including solar and other ecological requirements.
  • Capital spending. $54,600 for a sanitary sewer pump at the Chesterbrook Pump Station, plus $29,717 for surface-cleaning equipment and $24,056.40 for accessible door hardware at the library.
  • Library milestone. Friends of the Tredyffrin Library presented Library Director Mallory Hoffman with a $44,500 check; Township Engineer Stephen Burgo won a Valley Forge Trout Unlimited award for stormwater and environmental work.
  • Traffic concerns raised. Residents flagged speeding on Upper Gulph and King of Prussia Roads, plus traffic and student-housing issues in the Mt. Pleasant and Panhandle neighborhoods.
March 16, 2026

Public Safety Governance Parks & Recreation Infrastructure
  • Residents pressed for answers. The board addressed a March 7 incident at Contention Lane and Old State Road; resident Calvin Cizek requested “a follow up public forum” after residents raised concerns about communication gaps.

March 2, 2026
Official minutes (PDF) Video archive
Governance Public Safety Finance
  • A federal office lease drew a crowd. The township issued a statement on the Department of Homeland Security leasing space at 1000 West Lakes; six residents spoke against it over concerns about ICE activity, and the township solicitor noted Chester County’s position that local police won’t assist ICE except for public-safety matters.
  • Gun range and equipment. $199,255 approved for police gun range improvements, plus $30,735 for three new copiers and $58,455 for storm inlet tops.
  • Leaf blowers raised. Residents Scott Laird and Joanne Rose asked the board to consider an ordinance banning gas-powered leaf blowers.
  • Chase Road Park idea shelved. A resident-proposed community garden at 355 Chase Road was discussed but dropped for lack of support.
  • Other approvals. $9,800 for Friendship Park permitting, $13,121.92 for a sewer root-control program, and a resolution marking April as “PA 811 Safe Digging Month” (7–0).
  • Recognitions. Township Manager William Martin received a 35-year service award from ICMA; NovoCure and Metropolitan Veterinary Associates were recognized as Gold Level Sponsors of parks programming.
February 2, 2026
Official minutes (PDF) Video archive
Public Safety Finance Parks & Recreation Infrastructure
  • Police fleet expansion. The board approved $212,473 for four new police vehicles (one Tahoe, three Ford Interceptors) plus $77,527 in upfit costs, a separate $34,071.02 utility vehicle, and a $13,500 police drone — all unanimous.
  • Camera and tech upgrades. $42,379 for camera system upgrades at the Police Department, Township Building, and Paoli Library; $28,000 for new sewer/stormwater monitoring software; and up to $50,000 for phase 2 of records digitization.
  • Fire station heating swap. $133,000 was approved to convert the Fire Company substation on Valley Grove Road from oil to gas heat.
  • Environmental grants. The board backed a $100,000 Trout Unlimited grant application for Valley Creek restoration, and reallocated Bike Park Grant funds toward the Chase Road Park project.
  • Wilson Farm Park safety fix. A $12,745 change order was approved to bring the park’s tot lot gates up to safety code.
  • Residents pushed back. Roland Johnson and Richard Kunin questioned the pace of capital spending; several residents raised stormwater concerns tied to a proposed development at 317 Greene Street; and Johnson argued Chase Road Park should prioritize children’s equipment over pickleball courts.

How this would work as a real product

This prototype now covers seven real meetings (February–July 2026), built from the township’s official public minutes. A production version would need: (1) an automated pipeline that checks tredyffrin.org for new minutes after each meeting and generates the digest without manual work, (2) a simple way to get it in front of residents — email or text alert when a new digest is posted, rather than requiring them to check a website, and (3) eventually, coverage of more than one township, since every municipality in Chester County runs its own site and PDF format. None of that is a redesign of this concept — it’s the natural next build phase.
