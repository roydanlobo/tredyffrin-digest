Storylines to watch

Chase Road Park: costs climbing, questions mounting

This is exactly the kind of thread that’s easy to lose track of when each meeting’s minutes are read in isolation. Followed across several meetings, a clearer pattern emerges:

May 20 — $1.77M contract awarded → Jun 15 — Residents push back on cost & transparency; board postpones Change Order #1 (5–2) → Jul 20 — Residents flag accelerated timeline & rising costs again → Sep 21 & Oct 5 — No Chase Road items in the minutes
Open question: our Jul 20 digest doesn’t record how Change Order #1 was decided. The Oct 19 minutes aren’t posted yet.

Student rentals: from neighborhood complaints to a new ordinance

A complaint raised at the spring meetings reached a vote five months later:

Apr 20 — Residents flag traffic & student-housing issues in Mt. Pleasant and the Panhandle → May 20 — Supervisors commit to working with Villanova and updating enforcement ordinances → Aug 17 — Hearing set on HR–499; a resident says Chesterbrook rentals aren’t kept up → Sep 21 — HR–499 adopted, 7–0
To watch: how enforcement works in practice. The Sept 21 minutes don’t say when the new rules take effect.

Earned income tax: a motion with no second

A new thread from the most recent meeting. The idea is a township earned income tax paired with a real estate tax rollback.

Sep 21 — Motion to start work on an EIT and rollback gets no second and dies → Sep 21 — Four residents comment, from concern about an EIT to calls to cut expenses → Oct 5 — Not mentioned in the minutes
To watch: whether it returns. The Oct 19 minutes aren’t posted yet.

Home Rule Charter changes and the November ballot

Two ordinances amending the Home Rule Charter passed this spring, and the township says a referendum on the updates is on the November ballot.

Apr 20 — HR–495 adopted, 7–0 → May 20 — HR–496 adopted, 6–0 → Aug 17 — A resident asks how voters will learn about it; township says website, social media and e-newsletter → Oct 5 — A resident says voters still aren’t aware; supervisors promise an executive overview on Oct 19
Per the Oct 5 minutes, a red-lined version of the changes is on the township website, and the ballot wording was written by Chester County Voter Services. The Oct 19 overview isn’t in the minutes yet.

Recent meetings

October 5, 2026
Official minutes Video archive
Public Safety Finance Governance Infrastructure
  • Body camera contract renewed through 2031. The board unanimously approved a five-year renewal with Axon Enterprises for police body-worn cameras, starting in 2027: $80,254.91 in October 2027 (no increase over 2026), then $78,641.90 a year from 2028 through 2031. The cost is already budgeted and prices are locked in for five years. A resident raised concerns about an automated report-writing feature that comes with the product; police said the department isn’t using it.
  • Traffic software with privacy questions. The board unanimously approved a three-year contract with Urban SDK for traffic management software, $23,850 a year starting January 2027. Mr. Bhaskar raised privacy concerns and said IT and the vendor assured him the data can’t be tied to individuals; police said no license plate or personal data will be collected, and PDF copies of complaints will be viewable online. Onboarding begins before January 1.
  • Charter referendum awareness. A resident worried that voters don’t know about the November ballot referendum on updates to the Township Home Rule Charter. Staff listed their outreach: open houses, a website banner, social media, lobby postings, the printed newsletter and the e-newsletter. Supervisors committed to an executive overview at the October 19 meeting. Ms. Johnston-Pugh noted the open houses were poorly attended and pointed to a red-lined version of the changes on the township website, and Mr. van Mol said the ballot wording was written by Chester County Voter Services from township information.
  • Open space grant. The board adopted Resolution 2026-16, 7–0, authorizing an application to the 2026 PECO Green Region Open Space Program for the Airdrie Preserve project.
  • Winter salt costs more. The board approved the 2026–2027 salt contract with the lowest qualified bidder, Chemical Equipment Labs, up $7.73 a ton from last year. The minutes print the price as "$78l90", most likely $78.90 a ton. Asked about reserves, staff said there are none left from last year.
  • Employee dress code updated. The board unanimously approved updates to the Employee Dress Code Policy. The chief operating officer said many of the revisions were to use gender-neutral language.
  • Deer overpopulation. Ms. Humble raised a letter about deer overpopulation. Dr. Gosse said the township works with two deer management groups for culling but is limited in where they can work.
  • Public access to meetings. A resident asked why not every authority, board and commission meeting is on Zoom. Staff said the township can’t broadcast all of them: Board of Supervisors meetings are the only ones televised, and those and the Fire Commission’s are the only ones on Zoom. The same resident said some board and commission minutes are missing from the website and asked for faster posting.
  • Dates coming up. A conditional use hearing for Chabad Lubavitch of Chester County & Western Main Line is Oct 19 at 5:30 PM. The Fire Commission’s 2027 preliminary budget workshop is Oct 7 at 6 PM in Keene Hall, and the Arts Commission’s fall art show reception is Oct 9, 5–7 PM at the Township Building. The board also proclaimed Oct 4–10 Fire Prevention Week, 7–0. Township offices close Oct 12.
September 21, 2026
Official minutes (PDF) Video archive
Housing Finance Governance Infrastructure Public Safety
  • Student rental ordinance adopted. After a public hearing, the board voted 7–0 to adopt Ordinance HR-499, which deletes and replaces Chapter 151, Property Registration for Student Homes. The minutes don’t summarize the hearing testimony.
  • Earned income tax motion died for lack of a second. Mr. Bhaskar moved to direct staff to work with the solicitor on implementing an Earned Income Tax (EIT) paired with a real estate tax rollback, and described who would be affected. No supervisor seconded it, so it did not pass. Four residents spoke on the idea during public comment: one raised concerns, one said businesses locate in Tredyffrin partly because it has no EIT, one asked for more detail if the issue returns, and one criticized how the crowd treated the supervisors and asked them to look for ways to cut expenses.
  • Sewer capacity project. The board authorized submitting Pact Two’s proposal for the Devon Park Drive / 501 Swedesford Road sanitary sewer capacity enhancement project at $1,300,000, and approved a sewer agreement with 435 Devon Park Partners LLC. It also approved $25,955.25 to rebuild a pump at the Chesterbrook Pump Station.
  • West Circular Avenue stormwater push. The board approved Jacobs for engineering design at $280,000 for the West Circular Avenue / Spring Street stormwater and drainage project, and authorized staff to apply for a $2,900,000 PennDOT MTF grant, with a letter of support and financial commitment. Asked what happens if the grant is denied, staff said other grant sources exist.
  • Friendship Park moves toward bidding. The board approved $29,376.56 in additional design services from Simone Collins and Princeton Hydro and authorized staff to advertise for bids on the park improvements. Work could start by the end of the year. A resident asked why the project isn’t going through COSTARS, the state purchasing program.
  • Speed limit hearing set for Oct 19. The board authorized advertising an October 19, 2026 hearing on HR-498, which would amend Chapter 195 (Vehicles and Traffic) to change speed limits, stop intersections, no-parking zones and permit parking zones. A resident supported the changes but said they won’t do enough about speeding on Conestoga Road.
  • Two resolutions adopted 7–0. Resolution 2026-15 adopts the Chester County Hazard Mitigation Plan as Tredyffrin’s official plan. Resolution 2026-16 urges the Pennsylvania General Assembly to enact Extreme Risk Protection Orders (ERPO); Police Chief Beaty expressed support.
  • Other approvals. Audit: staff may issue an RFP for financial statement audits. Pensions: 2027 Minimum Municipal Obligations for the police and non-uniformed plans. Buildings: $18,043 for HVAC repairs at 1485 Valley Forge Road (a fire company official asked about mold; staff said remediation is underway). Vehicles: sale of four used township vehicles and a trade-in of a 2015 Ford Escape. Also a settlement agreement with Hunt Engineering and Brown and Caldwell, a lot line change for 920, 958 and 966 North Valley Forge Road, and a final $24,407.58 escrow release for 105 Vincent.
  • Appointments and recognition. Kate Hunsberger joins the Park & Recreation Board; Rob Croner and Kristin Dormuth were reappointed to the Libraries Board of Trustees; Jeff DuVouno of Brandywine Realty Trust joins the Comprehensive Plan Subcommittee. Corporal Chris Middleton was promoted to Sergeant and received an Award of Merit for a serious incident in August.
  • Process and other comments. Mr. Miller proposed requiring two cosponsors before an item goes on the agenda, and Dr. Gosse added that items should first go through the relevant committee. The Park & Recreation Board chair urged the supervisors to manage meetings better, possibly under Robert’s Rules of Order. A resident asked whether an ordinance on data centers is planned, and was told the supervisors are considering the issue. The next meeting is October 5.

Governance Housing Parks & Recreation Infrastructure
  • Rental upkeep concerns raised. A resident shared concerns about rental properties in Chesterbrook, saying landlords aren’t maintaining them to the same standard as neighboring homeowner-occupied properties.

  • Footbridge demolition concerns raised. Multiple Chesterbrook residents voiced opposition to Picket Post Swim Club’s planned demolition of a pedestrian footbridge near Bradford Road and asked supervisors to intervene; Mr. Miller said the township is organizing a meeting to discuss the issue.
  • Home Rule Charter information requested. A resident asked how information about the Home Rule Charter Amendment on the November ballot would be shared; Mr. Miller said the township will use its website, social media, and e-newsletter to educate voters.

How this would work as a real product

This prototype now covers ten real meetings (February–October 2026), built from the township’s official public minutes. A production version would need: (1) an automated pipeline that checks tredyffrin.org for new minutes after each meeting and generates the digest without manual work, (2) a simple way to get it in front of residents — email or text alert when a new digest is posted, rather than requiring them to check a website, and (3) eventually, coverage of more than one township, since every municipality in Chester County runs its own site and PDF format. None of that is a redesign of this concept — it’s the natural next build phase.
